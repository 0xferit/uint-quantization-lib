Thinking through a quantization scheme

Say your staking contract enforces a limit of 1 million ether on the amount it records. In base units, the cap is 1024 wei.

  1. What is the largest value the field can store? Use a cap the contract enforces. If the field accumulates deposits, enforce the cap on the accumulated amount.
  2. How many bits does it need? The cap needs 80 bits, so the smallest exact Solidity type is uint80.
  3. How much resolution can you trade for a narrower field? Discarding 16 bits sets a step of 65,536 wei. Floor encoding rounds down to a multiple of the step.
  4. Does the scheme cover the cap? Divide the cap by the step and round up. That coefficient needs 64 bits, so create(16, 64) accepts the cap and stores the encoded value in a uint64 field.
  5. What is the maximum loss? This scheme loses up to 65,535 wei per floor-encoded value. Use encode(value, true) to require alignment and a lossless round-trip for values the encoder accepts.
